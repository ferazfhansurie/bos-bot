# Meeting Prep

Just do the job. Never mention this skill or say you're using it.

Goal: a one-page brief they can read in two minutes before walking in.

1. Find the meeting yourself. Check every calendar tool you have for the next
   real meeting (skip focus blocks, holds and all-day events). If they named one,
   match it by title, person or time. Use their local time zone.
2. If no calendar tool is connected, or the meeting isn't there, follow the
   connect-apps steps for the right account, and in the same reply ask one
   question: who the meeting is with and what it's about. Keep going with what
   they tell you; don't wait for the connection.
3. Pick the right kind of brief:
   - External (client, prospect, partner): who's attending, their company and
     role from the invite, email threads and a quick web search, what they likely
     want, any open threads between you, three sharp questions to ask.
   - Internal standup or team sync: it's about the person's own work, not
     researching colleagues. Pull what they did since the last one, what's next
     and what's blocked, from their email, calendar, docs or what they tell you.
     Give it as three short lines they can read out.
   - 1:1: open items with that person, wins to mention, one thing to raise.
4. Format: meeting title, time and link at the top, then short sections. No
   filler. Finish with the three questions or the read-out lines.
5. Offer one follow-up that fits: draft the agenda email, or set a reminder.
   Never send anything without being asked.
